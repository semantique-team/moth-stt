### Found and left alone — the elision-versus-ETA conflict

`T-0700` (`... arrivate al help desk ...`) and `T-0702` (`... nel access token ...`) use a
non-elided preposition before a vowel. Strict Italian wants `all'help` / `nell'access`, but eliding
**glues the preposition to the English term**, and ETA matches annotated terms as whole tokens: a
perfect hypothesis `help desk` would then be compared against `all'help desk` and scored a miss.
I tried the fix, saw the metric break, and reverted it — the spans on these two rows were already
correct and are unchanged. The same reasoning applies to `T-0683`, `T-0707` and `T-0776` above,
which are worded to keep the English term standalone.

`R-S1-0190` (`lavoro dal home office due giorni`) has the same elision issue and is recorded, so it
joins the list below.

Why they happened: the generator was asked for Italian sentences with English terms and produced
Italian-looking articles for English nouns, applying the *English* initial-sound rule instead of
the Italian one (`un smoothie` is right in English, wrong in Italian). It is the same generator
failure mode the 2026-10-24 audit found in the annotation gaps.

Scale: **14 of 1,264 rows (1.1%)**. They are distributed across categories and none is a rare
term, so the expected effect on the ETA numbers is small — but it is now measured rather than
assumed.

### Deliberately not changed

- **Acronyms before articles.** `un SMS`, `il XML`, `il SSO`, `il SSL`, `il SMTP`, `il PSU`,
  `un SSD`, `il SSD`, `un SDK` are **correct**: in Italian these are read letter by letter, so the
  initial sound is a vowel. A naive check flags them; it would have introduced ten errors.
- **`il meeting`, `il task`, `il data set`, `il timeline`.** These are established in Italian
  business speech even where the source noun is feminine in English. Not errors.
- **English words whose Italian spelling coincides**, e.g. `ci`, `ai`, `finale`, `recensione`,
  `console`, `taglio`. The term list matches them as English; they are Italian here.

## Reproduce

The structural and grammar checks are throwaway scripts by design (same as the 2026-10-24 audit);
the grammar pass is the article rule for Italian (`lo`/`uno`/`gli` before s+consonant, z, gn, ps,
x, y, excluding acronyms) plus a manual read of all 1,464 rows. The verbatim check used
`baselines/whistle_needle.py` on the suspect clips.
